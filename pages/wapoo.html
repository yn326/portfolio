the company

Who is Wapoo?

Wapoo is a mobile social app targeting Gen Alpha users in the US, where people can use AI to warp and remix short-form videos, including “twisting” someone else’s video to give it a new ending, before sharing it within the app.

part 1

Wapoo’s visual style needed a redirection.

Wapoo’s MVP leaned into a bold, playful visual style to match Gen Alpha’s internet culture, but the result felt too busy and unpolished. I led a revamp of the app’s UI to strike a better balance between that energy and visual clarity.

How might we revamp Wapoo’s UI to keep its playful, Gen Alpha energy while making the experience feel clearer and more polished?

Competitor & secondary research

I reviewed 4 apps that had a similar visual style and audience.

After competitor research, I also reviewed early internal research on Gen Alpha’s UI preferences. A cleaner, pastel interface outperformed the boldest option, indicating that Gen Alpha doesn’t necessarily prefer maximalist design.

2 newer, distinctly Gen Alpha styled social apps

Bump

A consistent, recognizable style prevents a product from looking cheap.

ForReal

Express the Gen Alpha style through components instead of large gradients across the screens.

2 more established social/video apps

Tiktok

Clear hierarchy matters even more when the aesthetic is already bold and playful.

Instagram

Reduce color and highlights in the interface itself on media-heavy pages.

design decisions

I first defined a few requirements the new UI needed to meet.

01

Primary color: Blue

Since the logo remains unchanged, the primary color had to stay some form of blue for visual consistency.

02

Incorporate 3D elements

Since the logo itself is 3D, I aimed to bring 3D elements into the UI as well.

03

All designs should feel playful

Every redesign had to keep the quirky energy that is core to Wapoo’s identity.

04

Some designs should feel futuristic

As a nice-to-have, I explored futuristic themes in my designs, given that Wapoo is an AI product.

redesigns

Redesign 1 - Soft Neon

With my first redesign, I wanted to explore a direction that was clean but still playful, since our previous design had leaned too chaotic. I also experimented 3D elements in the UI to correspond with our 3D logo.

123

redesigns

Redesign 2 - Dark Neon

I explored the Soft Neon aesthetic in dark mode, leaning further into neon and glass details to push toward a more futuristic feel that reflects our AI-driven product. I also toned down on the elements that may have been too cutesy.

12

redesigns

Redesign 3 - Flat Pop

For my third redesign, I shifted away from glass and 3D textures, since both previous versions leaned heavily on them. I wanted to see if the playful theme could come through with a flat interface instead, using 3D only occasionally for CTAs.

12

redesigns

Redesign 4 - Cyber Pop

Redesign 3 didn’t work out, so I wanted to reintroduce texture and richness to the design. I also experimented with new storytelling elements — celestial, starry visuals and pixel art — to express something futuristic yet still playful.

12

choosing a design

The team chose the 4th redesign (Cyber Pop) as the final direction.

01

The team decided that dark mode better fits the app’s identity, narrowing the choice down to Redesign 2 (Dark Neon) and 4 (Cyber Pop).

02

The team felt Redesign 4’s starry, pixel-art details struck a better balance of playful and futuristic, while Redesign 2 felt less fun.

World Cup Fan War

design decisions

Onboarding Flow

iteration 1

More visually engaging since it pulls in each country’s colors.

Selecting countries other than the US requires swiping through ~40 countries.

Iteration 2

Uses a standard dropdown pattern, which makes selecting any country fast and familiar.

Less visually striking and more grayscale.

design decisions

Team Card

iteration 1

Text-based, slow to read, layout not engaging.

Iteration 2

Short, colored labels (Leading and Losing) lets users grasp their team’s status at a glance.

The “Leading + flag” message can be misread.

iteration 3

Added a label for the user’s own team to avoid confusion.

Removed statistics (eg. team total heat) from the card.

Iteration 4

Brought back missing statistics, uses hierarchy between info to avoid overload.

Removed the misleading “Leading + flag” phrasing.

iteration 5

Fixed info on left and changing stats on right, clear format to present information.

Less visually striking, weaker sense of competition

Iteration 6

Country flags in the background create sense of fighting for a team.

Tested an edge case with the longest country name to make sure the layout still held up.

design decisions

Trending Videos

The Trending Videos page ranks the 10 most popular videos on the app, encouraging users to create and engage with content in hopes of landing on the list. It is a permanent feature.

iteration 1

Larger video previews.

Cannot show creators, weakening incentive to create content.

Insufficient visual distinction between rankings (eg. 1 and 2).

Iteration 2

Shows name of creators, provides incentive for creators.

Every rank is distinct from the next.

Smaller video previews.

design decisions

Video Gallery

The Video Gallery displays videos posted or generated by Wapoo for each World Cup match.

iteration 1

Smaller area to display video covers.

No way to switch between dates.

Iteration 2

More space to display video covers.

Includes a way to switch between dates in case the user wants to know more about future matches or review past matches.
